# Open questions about NSK

In the NSK concept, everyone has a right to the base. Open questions concern how to implement that right and what consequences may follow. They do not imply means or usefulness tests.

| Question | What is known | What must be determined or tested |
|---|---|---|
| How can someone receive the base without an available employer? | The author expects charitable employers to develop | Whether that channel is sufficient, whether administration is available, and what happens if no such organisation exists |
| How can a payment interruption be avoided when a payer changes or closes? | The right belongs to the person | Who is responsible for the transfer, how records are kept and delays corrected |
| How is reimbursement provided when taxes are insufficient? | The rules provide full reimbursement | The procedure, cash-flow gaps and financing of settlements |
| How can duplicate payments be avoided? | One base per person per period | How to combine this rule with multiple jobs, representation of children and changes of payer |
| How can an entrepreneur receive the base without business activity? | Revision v2 specifies a personal credit | The procedure when no taxes are accrued has not been defined |
| What is the base amount and how does it change? | Only an illustrative ratio of 42 to 100 is given | A legislative programme must decide; the ratio does not define indexation |
| How does the base interact with pensions, benefits and services? | The concept does not automatically cancel them | Which payments remain and how total support changes |
| How is the system financed? | Gross bases under full coverage total `PB` | Which taxes and budget priorities to choose, how to transition, and what the selected option's net costs are |
| What happens to supplements, prices and employment? | Possible mechanisms of change are described | Data on behaviour, goods supply and market conditions, plus sensitivity checks on assumptions |
| Who is covered by a specific programme? | The base principle is universal | Jurisdiction and treatment of moves between countries; historical restrictions do not transfer automatically |

Until these questions are resolved, the principle and illustrative calculations can be discussed. But the absence of inflation, higher real incomes or budget neutrality cannot be treated as proven.

A useful objection identifies a specific rule, condition or consequence and considers both functions of NSK: supporting life and enabling earnings. Testing effects on employment is legitimate, but that test alone is not enough to assess the concept.
