# How the base is administered

The following describes proposed NSK rules. It is not a tax procedure currently in force.

1. Society establishes a personal base `B` and provides a tax credit with reimbursement.
2. The employer transfers the full base to the person and pays a work supplement `a`.
3. For `n` personal bases, the employer receives a credit of `nB` against taxes `T` accrued before the credit.
4. If `T − nB > 0`, the employer pays the balance. If `T − nB < 0`, it receives reimbursement of `nB − T`. If equal, the settlement balance is zero.

```text
Society / budget ── credit and reimbursement nB ──> employer
Employer ── full base B and supplement a ──> person
The personal right to B remains when the source of earnings changes.
```

The credit arrow represents compensation: a tax reduction and cash reimbursement together may cover the base. A separate transfer of the entire amount `nB` is not required.

With full compensation, the credit covers the base paid by the employer. The employer itself pays the work supplements. Equipment, work organisation and possible cash-flow gaps are separate costs. The accounting identity does not imply immediate reimbursement.

If the system covers `P` people, total bases for a period are `PB`. Part of this amount reduces tax receipts and part is reimbursed in cash. Each base is counted only once. The total `PB` does not determine the net budget change: that requires financing rules, data about previous payments and responses in the economy.

A child has a personal base even when the parent's employer administers it. A person may earn from multiple employers, but receives one base. The division of payment responsibility and transfer procedure remain open.

For people without ordinary work, the author expects a charitable channel to develop. NSK would provide the recipient's material resource; people and organisations would help arrange participation and administer payment.

## A family: two adults and two children

Take one illustrative monthly accounting period. A family has two working adults and two children. Each of the four members is entitled to base `B = 42`. Each adult's employer administers that adult's base and the base of the child represented by that adult. The state credits these amounts against employers' taxes only after the full amount has been transferred to the family.

With a supplement of 20 for each adult, the family receives `4 × 42 + 20 + 20 = 208`. The four personal bases total 168. From their own funds, the employers pay two supplements of 20 and separately bear organisational costs.

If the adults receive the same supplement for the same work, children do not give the employer an additional net gain: each child's base is fully transferred to the family and credited against taxes.
