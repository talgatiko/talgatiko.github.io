# Illustrative NSK examples

All amounts below are in illustrative monetary units for the same accounting period. Base `B = 42`; an average wage of 100 is used only as a starting scale. These amounts are not proposed for Russia.

The examples leave out personal taxes, work expenses and rules for other payments. They assume the person can access the payment and the employer receives full compensation.

| Case | Calculation | What it illustrates |
|---|---|---|
| One person, supplement 20 | `42 + 20 = 62` | The supplement increases income above the base |
| Part-time work, supplement 10 | `42 + 10 = 52` | The personal base does not depend on work hours |
| Two working adults and two children, two supplements of 20 | `4 × 42 + 20 + 20 = 208` | Four people receive four bases. The family receives 168; at equal supplements for equal work, children give the employer no net gain |
| One person with two jobs and supplements of 10 and 20 | `42 + 10 + 20 = 72` | There is one base; who administers it must be decided |
| No ordinary earnings, charitable payer available | `42 + 0 = 42` | This illustrates a proposed payment channel, not proof that it is available to everyone |

## Credit and reimbursement

For five recipients: `nB = 5 × 42 = 210`.

| Accrued taxes T | Balance T − nB | Settlement |
|---:|---:|---|
| 260 | 50 | Employer pays 50 to the state |
| 210 | 0 | Zero balance |
| 160 | −50 | Employer receives reimbursement of 50 |

In the last row, accrued taxes are reduced by 160 and the employer receives another 50 as reimbursement. Together this compensates 210. The budget cost of this credit is not 210 plus 50.

## Moving between payers

One person is entitled to 42 for one period. Changing employers does not create a right to two bases of 42. But uninterrupted payment during a transfer cannot yet be assumed: no procedure has been selected. Possible accounting methods include dividing the base by days or appointing one responsible payer; neither has been adopted in NSK.

## What happens to a previous wage of 100?

Both `42 + 58 = 100` and `42 + 100 = 142` preserve the full base. Only the supplement differs. The payment rule itself does not determine which outcome will occur and does not guarantee higher income.

If a benefit of 42 is withdrawn in full when a person starts work, earnings of 20 leave that person with income of 20, a loss of 22. With the NSK base retained, income is 62: work adds 20. This specific example from the [full article](/NSK/en/texts/nsk-003/) does not describe all benefit systems or compare total public costs.
