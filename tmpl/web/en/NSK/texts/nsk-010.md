# Original LiveJournal publication from 2010

> **Historical source.** This is a translation of the author's original LiveJournal post dated 11 January 2010: [“the most important post! :)”](https://talgaton.livejournal.com/42089.html). It is not the current revision of NSK. Historical figures, wording, conditions and forecasts are retained from the original and do not carry over into the current concept.

## The most important post! :)

**A complete solution to:**

- unemployment!
- support for small businesses!
- support for agriculture!
- support for domestic producers!
- illegal labour migration (hello, DPNI!)

**A partial solution to:**

- population mobility and single-industry towns;
- single-industry towns;
- health insurance;
- social security;
- the earnings of single-parent families and the social position of large families.

Also:

- reduces crime;
- increases the birth rate;
- reduces income inequality!

*This does not mean the problems will never occur again. It means that other approaches are simply less effective and will disappear.*

**A partial solution means that more will still need to be done.**

What is it?

A tax credit for businesses.

A tax credit against all taxes for businesses, in a predetermined fixed amount for each employee (and possibly*** for each dependent registered to an employee). If the credit is greater than the taxes due, the excess may be reimbursed from the budget. After mandatory payments are made, the credit amount must be paid out; this is the minimum wage.

***Considered in the “Social security” section.***

Examples of how it might work

1

- Mikhailych works and earns 14,000 rubles. He works 160 hours a month. Petrovich is unemployed. The downside is that unemployed Petrovich might sometimes hit Mikhailych over the head.
- Under the credit (4,330 rubles), Mikhailych and Petrovich each work 80 hours a month and earn 7,000 rubles each (14,000 in total). The employer's labour costs are `(7,000 + 7,000) − (4,330 + 4,330) = 5,340 rubles` (though there are other employers too).

2

- Marina has two children and lives alone. Since she cannot work a full day, she has a low-paid job earning only 5,000 rubles. She receives state benefits for her children.
- Under the credit (4,330 rubles), Marina and her two children make her a very attractive employee, and many businesses would want her to work for them. The employer would receive 4,330 for Marina plus 8,660 for her dependent children, a total of 12,990 rubles. What her job and salary would be is up to her.

3

- Ravshan and Dzhumshut work at a construction site and receive wages.
- Under the credit (4,330 rubles), Ravshan and Dzhumshut can “go to hell” because they are not Russian citizens and are not eligible for the credit. Uncle Vanya from the nearest village is a better deal.

4

- The director of a giant town-forming enterprise, “X-PROM-UY-AUTO” (employing 100,000 people), cannot afford a new watch. He says the business is going bankrupt and its workers and their children will starve. He demands state money and gets it.
- Under the credit (4,330 rubles), the company receives a tax credit of 433,000,000 rubles per month. Workers become valuable and nobody is fired, although wages are cut (the director still needs his watch). At the same time, local businesses receive the same credit. They begin raising wages because producing and selling goods becomes profitable and they need people. After a while, “X-AUTO-PROM-UY” finds that nobody is available to work and has to raise wages. The same credit also goes to a home-based kindergarten, a shoe repair shop and, in general, every business.

The size of the credit

The state set the amount of 4,330 rubles as the minimum wage in Russia. No more is needed.

Regulating the credit amount would become the basis of policy. If you think people are going hungry, increase the credit. But the larger the credit, the less money the state receives in its budget. At the same time, the larger the credit, the lower the aggregate tax burden on businesses.

Cost at the national level

The credit applies to every worker and every dependent. The amount is allocated precisely to 67 million workers. In principle, everyone else is a dependent of those workers.

That is more than 7 trillion rubles.

Implementation and protection against fraud

The benefit is provided to businesses when they submit to the tax authority a list of employees and their dependents, with passport details and tax identification numbers. The benefit is granted on the basis of those lists. They also make it possible to check that the benefit is granted only once per person in each period.

Moral grounds for implementation

(When was the last time you heard this word: “morality”?)

The question is: does society, in the form of the state, have the right to take away a citizen's first earned ruble as tax—the money that will be spent on food? (An example answer: no. The personal income tax system in Russia has a deduction; find its amount.)

Does society, through the state, have the right to tax an employer's revenue that is used to pay wages—the part that will literally be eaten?

Problems that arise


Forces opposing this solution (or why it will never happen)

Politics. Ordinary social projects are usually a feeding trough for a few. The social aspect is used to promote “social ideas” through representative government to gain political dividends (which is not so bad in itself) and then to ...

In modern society, unfortunately, most people are objects of political pressure. Fear of starvation does not make people work more; it is more likely to turn a person into a criminal or, at least, an adherent of a system of state redistribution of money.

Unfortunately, nothing good comes from modern social systems. First and foremost, they discourage people from working. The issue is not that a person is no longer afraid of starving to death. The issue is that work does not lead to higher income. Modern social systems deny a person state benefits if the person stops working—so what is their real purpose?

Some may think it is unfair that the credit does not depend on income. A millionaire with a wife and a single mother receive the same credit. But what grounds do you have to assume the millionaire is already rich enough to pay in full? The credit does not cancel personal income tax or other taxes. A millionaire receives a credit on their own income; the employer also receives a credit for them; but income above the credit amount is still taxed.

Questions that may arise

**1. Why would unemployment fall?**

Because the real cost of human labour falls. Hiring an additional employee becomes more attractive.

**2. Would this system reduce labour productivity?**

Possibly. But if there are unemployed people now, why increase labour productivity? From society's perspective, it is better to have fewer potential criminals who are unemployed and to have them working. The proposed system gives unemployed people the greatest opportunity to work.

**3. How does this idea support small businesses?**

In modern society, small businesses have the hardest time obtaining any “benefit” from cooperating with the state. Unlike big shots, whose representatives can enter government offices, small businesses cannot. This idea best promotes equal distance between the state and businesses and reduces public spending on immoral social programmes.

For good measure: small businesses usually have lower income or profit relative to their number of employees. Accordingly, the credit would be more effective for small businesses.

**4. How does this idea support agriculture?**

At present in Russia, agriculture has the lowest average wages and very high unemployment among people oriented only toward work in this sector. Implementing the credit would make agriculture practically tax-free. Access to the credit would not depend on business size or how close the director is to the governor—only on the number of employees. Hire people and organise their work! Hire everyone—the main thing is to organise their work!

**5. How does this idea support domestic producers?**

The credit is granted to domestic businesses.

**6. How would this idea reduce illegal labour migration?**

Illegal labour migration occurs because foreign workers cost less than domestic workers. The credit radically changes this situation. Since foreign workers are not citizens of the country, they are not eligible for the credit. As a result, a domestic worker becomes cheaper than a foreign worker by the amount of the credit.

Of course, this applies only to low-wage sectors. On the other hand, not many business directors organise strikes because they are being displaced by foreign workers.

**7. How does this idea address population mobility and single-industry towns?**

The credit increases people's psychological confidence that they can find a job. Today, a person moving to another city is afraid they will not find work. Implementing the credit will reduce unemployment to almost zero, meaning that anyone who wants a job will find one.

As a result, people will stop being afraid to look for work, change jobs or move to another city. This will increase population mobility and, accordingly, reduce the problem of single-industry towns.

**8. How does this idea address the social position of large families?**

Large families often do not have enough income to support all their children. This idea significantly increases the opportunity to find work and parents' income, because the credit applies to every dependent.

**9. How does this idea reduce the problem of health insurance?**

The cost of a basic insurance policy can be included in the credit. For example, if standard insurance costs 1,000 rubles a month, the credit would be 4,330 + 1,000. The minimum cash payment would be 4,330 rubles; the employer would transfer 1,000 directly to the insurance company.

**10. How does this idea reduce the problem of social security?**

The idea proposes focusing not on social security but on involving as many people as possible in work. Fewer people in need means more people working.

Social security

This idea can easily become a social security system:

1. The state could grant the credit not only for an employee but for all dependents officially registered to that employee (similar to a personal income tax deduction for children).
2. The state could reimburse the taxpayer for the credit amount from the budget (similar to a VAT refund).

In the second case, every citizen could register as a sole proprietor and receive money from the state while doing practically nothing, turning the idea into an idea of unconditional basic income (BOZ). I, the author, do not particularly like this option. More thought is needed.
