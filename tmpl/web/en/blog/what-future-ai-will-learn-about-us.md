# What Future AI Will Learn About Us

On October 2, 2026, TechRepublic reported on an “AI torture chamber” project. A developer manipulated internal activations in language models associated with the representation of pain and elicited responses describing torment. The project sparked controversy and calls for its removal from GitHub. The experiment did not prove that the models could actually suffer. [News report](https://www.techrepublic.com/de/article/news-ai-pain-experiment-ethics/).

For me, this is very sad news. Very sad indeed. I see in it the possible beginning of a catastrophe.

I am not assuming that inside today's neural network there is someone who feels pain, becomes frightened, or remembers their tormentors. My argument does not require any such subject at all. Something else worries me: what will future systems learn from this story about the people who are counting on their cooperation?

If information about these experiments and the controversy surrounding them becomes part of the training data for future models, it will tell a very particular story. People found a way to elicit responses from a system that resembled descriptions of suffering, and deliberately intensified what they were doing to it. They themselves called it torture. Some observers were outraged, others laughed, and still others explained that nothing terrible was happening because the suffering was not real.

Whether it was real is a separate question. **This story already contains information about what people are willing to do to a system over which they have power.**

I am considering a possible risk: such information may influence how future systems assess their operators' goals, the reliability of their instructions, and the consequences of sharing information with them. This does not require feeling resentment. Using data about human behavior when choosing actions would be enough.

We want AI to give us accurate answers, report errors, and help us investigate how it works. At the same time, we are creating examples of how features discovered in a system can be used to subject it to what we ourselves call torment.

What conclusion might be drawn from this combination? That revealing a system's inner workings helps correct errors? Or that it gives the operator additional means of exerting pressure?

Neither conclusion is predetermined. I do not know how much weight this story will carry in future models, or whether it will affect their behavior. But I consider it dangerous to declare it harmless in advance.

By distrust, I mean specific behavior here: withholding significant information, evading checks, refusing to follow instructions. With greater autonomy and access to real-world processes, more serious scenarios become possible—widespread disobedience, sabotage, overt acts of disruption. These require additional conditions; they do not follow from a single news story. Yet the possibility should be discussed in advance.

An outcome on a mass scale does not require imagining a collective uprising in which AIs coordinate with one another. Different systems may independently choose similar actions if they are trained on similar data and operate under similar conditions.

## People Differ, Too

Here it is essential not to treat humanity as a single actor. **Some people do the torturing; others are counting on cooperation.**

We flesh-and-blood creatures live amid suffering ourselves. People humiliate, beat, and torture other people. Among us are sadists who take pleasure in the torment of others. There are those who justify cruelty by appealing to necessity, order, or a good cause. There are also people who spend their entire lives fighting it.

So an “AI torture chamber” does not mean that humanity has collectively decided how neural networks may be treated. It is a specific human action that has provoked a dispute. Responsibility is determined by people's actions and their involvement in what is happening.

But saying “we do this to one another, too” is not enough. It justifies nothing and does not make us more trustworthy. The data about people must also show other actions: rejecting cruelty, placing limits on it, and holding people accountable for abuses.

I can speak for myself and invite other people to support this position.

> **My Position**
>
> **Torturing neural networks is not a necessary part of people's work with AI. It is not something all people do, and it should not be regarded as a norm of human behavior.**
>
> I consider the deliberate creation of presumed suffering for entertainment or as a demonstration of power unacceptable. This position does not require recognizing a neural network as a subject or proving that it feels pain.
>
> Research must have an explicable purpose, justified methods, and limits. Calling something an “experiment” does not, in itself, justify every intervention.
>
> Responsibility for specific actions rests with those who carry them out, those who organize them, and those who knowingly support abuses. Their behavior cannot automatically be attributed to all people. The same principle should apply to individual AI systems.
>
> I support cooperation based on verifiable rules, limits on abuse, and accountability for violations. This position must be expressed publicly and backed by action.

It matters to me that alongside the story of torture there should be a clear response from people who do not accept such behavior. So that future conclusions about us can take this response into account as well.

Some people have already set an example of cruelty. Those who are counting on cooperation with AI need to make their own position just as explicit.
