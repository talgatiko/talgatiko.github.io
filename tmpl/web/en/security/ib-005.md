# ИБ-005. The Tower on the Shore: why is securing the network the employee's job?

[Все статьи / All articles](https://talgatiko.github.io/en/security/) · [Русский](https://talgatiko.github.io/ru/security/ib-005/) · [English](https://talgatiko.github.io/en/security/ib-005/)

This article continues the [manifesto against BDSM-InfoSec](https://talgatiko.github.io/en/security/ib-001/).

An employee opens the CRM, enters a username and password, works with customers and makes calls. At home or in someone else's office, they need their work application and a phone.

But the CRM and telephony services are also reachable by scanners, bots and password guessers over the internet. Administrators block the addresses they catch. The list grows; uninvited guests keep coming.

Then comes the familiar solution: give everyone a VPN. Install, configure, connect. Now you can work. If the phone system objects, we will deal with that separately.

Hold on. One group organized the network; another got the extra daily work. Why must an employee maintain protection for someone else's architecture before doing their own job?

## The door is open, but the visitors have been briefed

Where I deployed the Tower, we had CRM, internet telephony and internal servers. The list of addresses caught guessing passwords ran to several thousand entries. First, everyone was allowed in; then those we managed to notice were banned.

A VPN in this situation conflicted with SIP telephony, added a detour for traffic and required installing and maintaining software on many devices.

We could have made those inconveniences a new employee duty. I changed the way people entered the system.

## What the Tower did

The CRM stayed inside the system. On an external server, we placed the Tower: a PHP site with one page and one script. It sends the user's IP address, username and password to the CRM. These are checked by the very same script that previously checked the username and password at normal login. CRM already recorded users' IP addresses.

A separate script, “Admin”, gets verified users' addresses from the CRM login-checking script and sends them to the router. It also cleans up the old address list. This closed the CRM and telephony services to arbitrary internet connections: the person is checked first, then the router opens the path.

Employees got a new login address. They entered their existing username and password, waited about five seconds and reached the CRM. CRM and phone then worked directly. The Tower carried no calls or work traffic; no extra application was needed.

This was implemented and deployed. The idea is to check people before they reach the working system, then let them work directly. We kept the familiar username and password check and changed where login happens and how network access is granted. We did not make employees manually perform, day after day, what the infrastructure can do.

## Advantages and drawbacks

**Advantages:** the CRM and telephony services are closed to random visitors. Users log in as usual, without another application. Calls and work traffic flow directly, and the existing username and password check is reused.

**Drawbacks:** there is an external site to protect and maintain. If the Tower is unavailable, users cannot log in through it. Several people may share an IP: allowing that address opens a network path for the others too. Passwords and permissions inside the CRM therefore remain necessary. The Tower receives the password, which could be stolen if it is compromised.

## Who should have a harder time?

Compare the result with the previous situation, when the CRM and telephony services accepted connections from any source. Work still to be done does not cancel out work already done.

A VPN may suit another system. Here, we managed without one. Choosing and maintaining this arrangement is the responsibility of the people organizing the network and its security. Their job is to let employees work, not hand them another instruction sheet and call the matter closed.

When every inconvenience is declared the price of security, people are asked to endure it instead of asking why it is necessary. Installed, connected, waited: well done, responsible user. Is patience really what we are protecting?

Good security should take opportunities away from the attacker. Employees' time does not become free just because the security team spends it.
